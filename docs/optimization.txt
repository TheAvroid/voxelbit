Perform a deep, measurement-driven optimization pass on voxelbit.html.

This is a large single-file WebGPU voxel engine, not a conventional webpage. Make real implementation changes directly in the file. Do not merely provide recommendations.

PRIMARY GOALS

Improve:

1. Average FPS and GPU frame time.
2. Frame-time consistency and 1% lows.
3. Main-thread simulation cost and garbage collection.
4. Procedural terrain generation and streaming throughput.
5. Startup time.
6. CPU/GPU memory usage and resource lifetime.
7. Performance during snowfall, water rendering, dense wildlife, rapid movement, and terrain streaming.

NON-NEGOTIABLE CONSTRAINTS

Preserve all existing:

- Visual quality and voxel aesthetic.
- Fixed 10 cm voxel scale.
- Current resolution scale and render distance.
- Physically based water, reflections, refraction, Beer–Lambert absorption, foam and Gerstner waves.
- Lighting, AO, SVGF/temporal filtering, TAA, shadows and cinematic effects.
- Terrain appearance, caves, rivers, vegetation and deterministic generation.
- Creature behavior, animation timing, voxel models and collision behavior.
- Snow accumulation, freezing and melting behavior.
- Editor, recorder, controls, HUD and menus.
- Endless toroidal-world streaming.
- Existing diagnostic and recovery systems.
- Single-file HTML architecture with no new dependencies or build step.

Do not gain performance by silently lowering resolution, draw distance, creature counts, simulation frequency, world dimensions, water quality, ray counts or effect quality. Any intentional approximation must be visually indistinguishable, demonstrated by an A/B comparison, and documented.

PHASE 1 — ESTABLISH A BASELINE

Before changing code, inspect the complete architecture and instrument the actual hot paths.

Use the existing GPU timestamp-query profiler exposed through __vb.prof(true), but extend profiling where necessary. Add lightweight CPU timing telemetry for major tick phases without leaving expensive profiling enabled during normal play.

Measure at minimum:

- Stationary view at spawn.
- Normal walking.
- Sustained sprinting while terrain streams.
- Grazing-angle view across a large body of water.
- Dense creature/life scene.
- Active snowfall and snow accumulation.
- Asset-editor mode.
- Video-editor idle and recording modes.
- 90% and 100% resolution.

Record:

- Total CPU frame time.
- GPU time for trace, temporal, spatial, composite and TAA.
- Terrain-generation worker time.
- Main-thread terrain integration/upload time.
- Number and total bytes of GPU uploads per frame.
- Per-frame allocations and garbage-collection pressure.
- Peak CPU memory and estimated GPU memory.
- Frame-time spikes and their causes.

Do not optimize from assumptions. Use the measurements to rank bottlenecks.

PHASE 2 — GPU OPTIMIZATION

Audit the WGSL and compute pipeline carefully, especially:

- Primary voxel DDA traversal.
- L1 8³ and L2 32³ occupancy traversal.
- Water reflection/refraction secondary rays.
- AO and shadow rays.
- Dynamic-life model DDA.
- Creature visibility masks and per-tile culling.
- Trace, temporal, spatial, composite and TAA bandwidth.
- Repeated world, occupancy and palette reads.
- Branch divergence and long fixed-bound loops.
- Workgroup sizes and texture formats.
- Full-resolution passes that could be fused without changing output.
- Recomputed values that can be shared or precomputed.
- Excessive rgba16float traffic or unnecessary intermediate textures.

Pay particular attention to the expensive grazing-water case and to pixels with no water, creatures, snow or held items. Ensure those pixels do not pay for irrelevant work.

Consider pass fusion, tighter bounds, early exits, conservative screen/tile bounds, cached common expressions and improved traversal only when they preserve output and temporal-history correctness.

Do not weaken the existing occupancy system or introduce missed voxels, light leaks, water holes, disappearing creatures, unstable shadows or temporal artifacts.

PHASE 3 — CPU AND SIMULATION OPTIMIZATION

The monolithic tickBody contains player physics, streaming, weather, snow, wildlife, animation, sorting, uniform packing and rendering preparation. Profile each subsystem separately.

Look for:

- Temporary arrays, objects and closures allocated every frame.
- Repeated Math.sin, Math.cos, Math.hypot, modulo and hash work.
- Full-array clears or scans.
- Per-frame Array.sort calls where fixed-size selection or insertion is cheaper.
- Repeated terrain, height-map or collision queries.
- Life AI that can be updated on stable fixed-rate clocks while interpolation remains render-rate smooth.
- Inactive or distant entities doing unnecessary work.
- Snow landing scans repeated for the same columns.
- Duplicate scanTop calls during snow settling.
- Small GPU updates issued as many separate queue.writeBuffer calls.
- Rebuilding occupancy more broadly than the modified voxel region requires.
- Hot use of Sets, Maps, spreads, object creation or general-purpose collections where pooled typed arrays would be better.

Reuse storage and typed arrays where practical. Avoid introducing correctness-sensitive object pooling unless the benefit is measured.

Animation, physics, AI and weather must remain time-based and must not change speed with render FPS.

PHASE 4 — TERRAIN STREAMING AND UPLOADS

Audit the worker-pooled generator, genBandGen, stepShifts, strip scatter, occupancy rebuilding and GPU uploads.

Investigate:

- poolQueue.shift() and other avoidable queue movement.
- Worker scheduling, load balance and chunk dimensions.
- Redundant model stamping across worker-region boundaries.
- Worker serialization and transferred data volume.
- Main-thread slab integration.
- Rebuilding or uploading entire bricks, bricks2 and wbricks arrays after localized bands.
- Z-band writeBuffer volume.
- Repacking X strips on the main thread.
- One-scatter-in-flight restrictions.
- Shared staging-buffer serialization.
- Generator wait/spin behavior.
- The effect of the 7–18 ms foreground generation budget on frame-time spikes.
- Whether dirty ranges or dirty-word lists can replace broad uploads.
- Whether multiple tiny writes can be coalesced safely.

Preserve the critical ordering around xStripPending. Do not reintroduce stale scatter data, floating grey terrain, toroidal seams or CPU/GPU world divergence.

PHASE 5 — RESOURCE AND MEMORY LIFETIME

Audit every GPU buffer, texture, view, bitmap, Blob URL, worker and media resource.

makeTargets currently recreates a large collection of render textures and bind groups when resolution or canvas size changes. Ensure superseded textures and buffers are explicitly destroyed only after they are no longer in GPU use. Debounce resize-driven recreation and avoid rebuilding targets when dimensions have not actually changed.

Also inspect:

- The replaced one-pixel moon fallback texture.
- ImageBitmap lifetime.
- MediaRecorder streams and tracks.
- Object URLs.
- Worker termination/failure paths.
- Profiling buffers.
- Video-editor canvas and recording resources.
- Large temporary terrain-generation allocations.

Do not destroy a resource while submitted GPU work may still reference it.

PHASE 6 — PACING AND SYNCHRONIZATION

Inspect the non-vsync pacing based on device.queue.onSubmittedWorkDone().

Determine whether it unnecessarily serializes CPU and GPU work or reduces throughput. Improve pacing only if measurements show a benefit, while preserving:

- Uncapped mode.
- Vsync mode.
- Hidden-tab idling.
- Terrain-generation idle-time budgeting.
- Device-loss and rejected-promise recovery.
- Prevention of runaway command submission.

Do not remove freeze guards or error diagnostics.

CORRECTNESS REQUIREMENTS

After every substantial change, verify:

- __vb.gpudiff() reports zero differences inside the generated rectangle.
- __vb.gtest() reports zero pool-versus-inline terrain differences.
- __vb.btest() reports no occupancy errors.
- __vb.htest() reports zero height-generation differences.
- Terrain remains deterministic at identical world coordinates.
- No streaming seams appear at toroidal boundaries.
- No stale occupancy or floating terrain appears.
- Collision and voxel edits update both CPU and GPU state.
- Temporal history resets correctly after resize, teleport and editor transitions.
- Dynamic creatures do not ghost, disappear or inherit another creature’s history.
- Water does not develop holes, missing reflections or unstable edges.
- Snow still accumulates, settles, freezes and melts correctly.
- Entering and leaving the asset editor remains correct.
- Recording/export behavior remains smooth.
- Device loss and transient render errors cannot permanently stop the loop.

IMPLEMENTATION POLICY

Work incrementally:

1. Baseline and rank bottlenecks.
2. Apply one coherent optimization group.
3. Re-measure.
4. Retain it only if it produces a measurable improvement or fixes a verified leak.
5. Continue with the next bottleneck.

Do not perform a broad aesthetic refactor. Do not rename thousands of symbols, minify the file, remove useful comments or rewrite proven systems without evidence. Keep the source understandable.

Add concise comments explaining non-obvious optimizations, ordering constraints and correctness invariants.

FINAL DELIVERABLE

Return the fully optimized HTML file and a concise report containing:

- Baseline versus final CPU and GPU timings.
- FPS and frame-time changes for every test scenario.
- Startup and terrain-streaming improvements.
- Memory and allocation improvements.
- Exact bottlenecks found.
- Exact optimizations implemented.
- Verification results from the existing diagnostic functions.
- Any remaining bottlenecks that could not safely be removed.

The goal is not “cleaner code.” The goal is substantially faster execution with demonstrably identical behavior and image quality.
