"Graphical upgrades — recommendations only
Costs are rough fractions of current frame time on the tracer, given this is now shipping to players' own GPUs. I've left out anything the project already tried and rejected.

Near-free (<2%), high payoff

Foliage translucency. Needles lit from behind currently go flat dark. One extra term — light through the leaf, scaled by view·sun — transforms a backlit pine. Best value on this list for a forest game.
Wet-sand darkening at the shoreline. Albedo darkened by distance to the waterline. No new rays, no texture; makes beaches read as wet.
Multiple-scattering atmosphere LUT. Precompute the sky table once at load; runtime becomes a texture fetch instead of the current analytic gradient. Richer dawn/dusk for essentially nothing per frame.
Bevelled voxel edges. A small SDF-based rounding in the shading normal only, no geometry. Takes the hard flatness off every cube.
Cheap (2–5%)

Per-material specular (GGX) for stone, ice and wet surfaces. The world is nearly all diffuse right now; a proper specular lobe on the sun would separate rock from soil from ice.
Emissive bloom. Fireflies, lava and the sun glints on water get a real glow. A few downsample/upsample passes.
Bent normals from the AO ray. You already fire one stratified AO ray; accumulating its direction gives a bent normal for free-ish and makes sky lighting sit correctly in crevices.
Screen-space contact shadows. A short ray to tighten where creatures and objects meet the ground — the one place the shadow currently detaches.
Moderate (5–15%)

One-bounce indirect at quarter resolution, reprojected through the existing denoiser. Real colour bleed — green under the canopy, sand bounce on a beach. The single biggest step up in image quality available, and the most expensive on this tier.
Depth of field, cinematic mode only, so it costs nothing during play.
Shadow-sampled volumetrics. Upgrade the existing volumetric term to sample the sun shadow along the march — proper god rays through the canopy. Attractive, but it's the kind of cost that hurts a weak GPU most.
I'd skip

Anything relying on render-scale reduction to pay for the above — that trades sharpness for effects, which cuts against your standing "perceptually lossless" rule. Worth having as a user-facing option, not as a default.
Given the local-browser shipping constraint, the first tier plus specular and bloom would visibly lift the game for a few percent of frame time. The one-bounce indirect is the one worth prototyping behind a toggle and measuring before committing."
